Section 01

Situation & impact: what's affected, and what isn't

In mid-June 2026, security researchers disclosed a large-scale credential-exposure campaign nicknamed “FortiBleed.” Understanding it correctly is the first step to a calm, effective response.

Despite the “-bleed” nickname, this is not a Heartbleed-style memory bug and not a new software flaw in FortiOS. Independent researchers reported that attackers harvested configuration backup files from FortiGate devices whose management or SSL-VPN interface was reachable from the public internet, then cracked the password hashes stored inside those files offline — producing working administrator and VPN credentials.67

Fortinet's official PSIRT analysis is direct: the activity “is not a new Fortinet vulnerability” and is not related to any recent advisory. Fortinet attributes it to threat actors reusing credentials from prior incidents (referencing advisories FG-IR-26-060 and FG-IR-25-647) combined with brute-force against devices with weak password hygiene and no multi-factor authentication (MFA).1 In other words, the exposed asset is access — not a patchable code defect.

That distinction matters for how you respond. There is no fix to “wait for.” A device that is fully up to date but still uses old, crackable password hashing — and is reachable from the internet without MFA — remains exposed until its credentials are rotated and its access is locked down. Equally, a device whose management and VPN are already restricted to trusted networks with MFA enforced is largely outside the initial harvest scope.7

Quick self-check: “Am I exposed?”

If you answer “yes” to all three, treat administrator and VPN credentials on that device as potentially exposed and proceed to Section 02.

1Internet-facing? Is the FortiGate's admin interface (HTTPS/SSH) or SSL-VPN portal reachable directly from the public internet?
2Legacy hashing? Were the admin / local VPN passwords last set on a build older than 7.2.11 / 7.4.8 / 7.6.1, or before each admin logged in after upgrading?
3No second factor? Are any administrator or VPN accounts without MFA?

On the numbers

Third-party estimates of impacted devices range widely — roughly 30,000 to ~86,000 internet-facing devices across 194 countries, with some researchers suggesting about half of the internet-exposed FortiGate fleet.67 Treat these as directional, not a measure of your own risk. Your exposure depends entirely on the three checks above.

Section 02

Immediate containment & workaround

There is no patch to wait for — containment is about access hygiene, and you can act today. The six steps below follow Fortinet's official guidance,1 reinforced by CISA5 and consultancy sources.67

1

Terminate sessions & reset all credentials

End all active administrative and SSL-VPN sessions. Reset every Fortinet administrator, local user and VPN password — prioritising internet-facing systems — and enforce a strong password policy.

Source · Fortinet PSIRT, CISA
2

Enforce MFA everywhere

Turn on multi-factor authentication for all administrator and VPN accounts. This is the single most durable control: a reused password has no second factor to defeat. Phishing-resistant factors are preferred where available.

Source · Fortinet PSIRT, CISA, Arctic Wolf, Bitdefender
3

Reduce attack surface — lock down management

Take management and SSL-VPN off the open internet. This is the highest-impact mitigation and takes effect immediately, before any upgrade. See the good / better / best tiers below.

Source · Fortinet PSIRT, Bitdefender
4

Validate the configuration

Review firewall, VPN users and settings for unauthorised changes — ideally against a known-good backup. Watch for unfamiliar accounts named like forticloud, fortiuser, fortinet-support, fortinet-tech-support.

Source · Fortinet PSIRT
5

Check your logs

Look for administrator logins from unknown IPs/geographies, configuration-export events to external addresses, unexpected SSL-VPN logins, and domain-controller logs that suggest lateral movement.

Source · Fortinet PSIRT, Bitdefender
6

If you find an indicator — treat as compromised

Follow Fortinet's device-recovery guidance, treat any integrated AD/LDAP account as compromised, monitor for lateral movement, and engage FortiGuard Incident Response for a scoping call.

Source · Fortinet PSIRT

The workaround that matters most: get management off the internet

Fortinet frames management lock-down as good / better / best. Any tier is a valid emergency workaround; aim to reach “best.”1

Good

Restrict by trusted hosts

Limit each admin account to known management source IPs (trusthost), so the login surface only answers your networks.

Better

Local-in policy

Apply a local-in policy that permits management/VPN only from defined trusted sources and drops everything else at the device itself.

Best

Remove internet administration entirely

No administrative or SSL-VPN exposure on the WAN at all — manage out-of-band or via FortiManager, and move remote access to ZTNA.

!

Don't stop at “patched”

Patching related CVEs is good hygiene but does not close this exposure. Valid credentials survive firmware upgrades — the credential window only closes when each credential is rotated and MFA is in place.7

Section 03

Technical remediation — firmware, configuration & CLI

For the customer's network / security team. Precise steps to migrate hashing, rotate credentials, enforce MFA and shrink the attack surface. Validate exact syntax against your running FortiOS version before applying in production.

Root cause in one paragraph

FortiGate stores administrator and local-user passwords as hashes inside the configuration. Older builds used salted SHA-256 — fast, and therefore crackable offline with commodity GPU hardware. PBKDF2 (deliberately slow, offline-cracking-resistant) was introduced in FortiOS 7.2.11, 7.4.8 and 7.6.1. Two subtleties make the migration incomplete by default: (1) after an upgrade, an existing admin's password stays SHA-256 until that admin logs in again; and (2) the original SHA-256 hash is retained in a hidden old-password field for backward compatibility until it is explicitly purged.67

Step 1 · Firmware targets

Upgrade to a build that supports PBKDF2 — Fortinet recommends the latest 7.4, 7.6 or 8.0 (minimum 7.2.11 / 7.4.8 / 7.6.1). Devices below these builds store all passwords as SHA-256 with no migration path until upgraded. Plan the path with the Fortinet Upgrade Path tool.8

Minimum builds for PBKDF2 support
7.2 branch≥ 7.2.11
7.4 branch≥ 7.4.8
7.6 branch≥ 7.6.1
Fortinet recommendationLatest 7.4 / 7.6 / 8.0

Step 2 · Migrate hashing to PBKDF2 & purge legacy hashes

After upgrading, require every administrator to log in at least once — this converts their hash to PBKDF2. Then purge the retained SHA-256 material. On 7.2.x / 7.4.x, enable the documented setting:46

FortiOS CLI · enforce PBKDF2 / purge old-password
# after every admin has logged in once post-upgrade:
config system password-policy
    set login-lockout-upon-weaker-encryption enable
end
i

Why both steps are needed

The upgrade alone does not replace existing hashes; the per-admin login does the conversion, and the setting above removes the leftover SHA-256 in old-password. Do them in that order.

Step 3 · Rotate credentials & enforce a strong policy

FortiOS CLI · reset an admin password (run as super_admin)
config system admin
    edit "<admin-name>"
        set password <new-strong-secret>
    next
end
FortiOS CLI · representative strong password policy
config system password-policy
    set status enable
    set min-length 14
    set min-lower-case-letter 1
    set min-upper-case-letter 1
    set min-number 1
    set min-non-alphanumeric 1
    set expire-status enable
end

Step 4 · Restrict management to trusted hosts

FortiOS CLI · per-admin trusted hosts
config system admin
    edit "<admin-name>"
        set trusthost1 10.0.0.0 255.255.255.0   # your mgmt subnet
        set trusthost2 <jump-host-ip> 255.255.255.255
    next
end

Step 5 · Remove administrative access from the WAN

FortiOS CLI · drop admin/VPN services on the internet-facing interface
config system interface
    edit "<wan-interface>"
        # keep only what you truly need; remove http/https/ssh admin from WAN
        set allowaccess ping
    next
end
# Better: enforce with a local-in policy permitting mgmt only from trusted sources,
# and deny all other inbound to the device's management services.

Step 6 · Enable MFA on admin & VPN accounts

FortiOS CLI · two-factor with FortiToken (example)
config system admin
    edit "<admin-name>"
        set two-factor fortitoken
        set fortitoken <token-serial>
        set email-to <admin@bank.example>
    next
end

At scale, use FortiAuthenticator for centralised MFA and FortiToken provisioning across the fleet; for remote workforce access, prefer FortiClient + ZTNA over an always-listening SSL-VPN portal.

Detection & threat-hunting (what to look for)

›Configuration-backup export events to an external IP — a strong signal of harvesting.
›Administrator authentication from unfamiliar IPs or geographies, or at unusual times.
›New administrator accounts created outside your normal change-control workflow.
›Changes to logging, authentication or interface settings outside documented windows.
›Outbound tunnels to unrecognised IPs, or SSL-VPN logins for known users from impossible locations.
i

Related CVEs to patch (context, not the root cause)

FortiCloud SSO weaknesses that helped seed earlier credential capture — CVE-2026-24858 (patched Jan 2026), CVE-2025-59718 and CVE-2025-59719 (patched Dec 2025). Patch them as good hygiene, but remember: rotation + MFA + access restriction is what closes the credential exposure.7

SE talk-track · how to frame this with the customer's technical team

“The good news is there's nothing to wait for — the fix is access hygiene, and it uses tools you already own. We can roll the configuration baseline across your whole fleet from FortiManager, push MFA centrally with FortiAuthenticator and FortiToken, and validate clean-up by hunting the FortiAnalyzer logs for config-export and off-geo admin events.”

Positioning, not promises: anchor on what the customer controls today (rotate, MFA, lock down management), then show how FortiManager (fleet roll-out), FortiAnalyzer / FortiSIEM (hunting), FortiAuthenticator (MFA at scale) and ZTNA (retire exposed SSL-VPN) make it faster and durable. Avoid committing to remediation timelines or outcomes on Fortinet's behalf — direct binding questions to the account team and FortiCare.

Section 04

Staying protected & responding faster

FortiBleed is a hygiene wake-up call. The same controls that contain it also raise the cost of the next incident and shorten your response time.

Four durable shifts

PillarMake it the standardWhy it shortens the next response
ArchitectureManagement off the public internet for good — out-of-band or FortiManager-only; migrate remote access from SSL-VPN to ZTNA; segment the identity tier.Removes the internet-wide scan surface that this campaign — and the next one — starts from.
IdentityMFA everywhere, least-privilege admin, central authentication (FortiAuthenticator / AD with MFA), routine credential rotation, encrypted config-backup handling.A leaked credential is no longer enough on its own; rotation bounds its useful life.
Hygiene baselineStandardise on PBKDF2, keep firmware on supported builds, and treat every configuration backup as a secret.Closes the offline-cracking path before attackers can use it.
VisibilityCentralise logs to FortiAnalyzer / FortiSIEM; alert on config-export, off-geo admin logins and new admin accounts; know what is internet-exposed.Turns “did something happen?” into a same-day answer instead of a forensic project.

A 30-day hardening roadmap

Now · 0–24h
Contain. Terminate sessions, rotate admin/VPN credentials, enable MFA, restrict management to trusted hosts. (Section 02.)
72 hours
Verify & migrate. Confirm every admin has logged in to convert to PBKDF2, purge legacy hashes, audit config against a known-good baseline, and review logs for indicators.
30 days
Re-architect. Remove internet administration entirely, stand up centralised MFA, and begin the SSL-VPN → ZTNA migration. Roll the baseline fleet-wide via FortiManager.
Ongoing
Monitor & rehearse. Subscribe to FortiGuard outbreak alerts and Fortinet PSIRT, keep an incident runbook with a FortiGuard IR contact, and re-check your external attack surface on a schedule.

A note for banking customers

Banks are high-value targets precisely because a recovered edge credential can become a path toward identity systems and, ultimately, core banking. The controls in this brief — strong authentication / MFA, privileged-access control, comprehensive logging and retention, and timely incident handling — map cleanly to the supervisory expectations regulated institutions already work to in Cambodia (NBC) and Myanmar (CBM).

This brief does not interpret specific regulatory obligations. Coordinate exact control mappings, evidence and any notification requirements with your compliance function and your respective regulator.
